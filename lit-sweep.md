# Backtracking in Latent Reasoning Models: A Literature Map for a "Revisit vs. Override" Project

## TL;DR
- **Latent "backtracking" is established mostly by readout, not by causal mechanism.** The headline existence claim (Cui & Ye 2026, arXiv 2602.08100) rests entirely on decoded beliefs; the strongest causal work (Dynamics within Latent CoT, 2602.08783; Observable Patterns, 2606.12689; SCIT, 2608.27265) shows non-local/routed dependence and shortcut behavior but does **not** test "revisit vs. override," and Observable Patterns argues the BFS/superposition readouts also appear in non-latent controls.
- **"Revisit" is architecturally near-impossible in the two flagship depth-recurrent models.** In Huginn-3.5B and Ouro the per-token recurrent state is a fixed-size vector overwritten each iteration, so a later loop cannot attend back to a token's earlier latent state. Ouro retains per-loop KV across sequence positions (grows linearly); Huginn tolerates cyclically overwriting it — so most "latent backtracking" is almost certainly **override**, not revisit.
- **The "revisit vs. override across architectures" project is genuinely novel and uncrowded; a "latent backtracking direction" project is more crowded.** The architectural asymmetry gives the revisit/override framing a clean, publishable causal test; a standalone direction paper risks being seen as a port of existing CoT-side steering work.

## Key Findings

1. **No existing paper distinguishes "revisit" from "override" in latent models.** Every latent-side paper either (a) decodes beliefs and calls a reversal "backtracking" without testing whether an earlier latent state is retrieved (Cui & Ye), or (b) intervenes step-wise and finds non-local/routed influence but frames it as "which step is causally necessary," not "does a later step retrieve an earlier one." This is the clean gap.

2. **The architecture answer strongly biases toward "override."** A token's own earlier-iteration latent in Huginn (arXiv 2502.05171) and Ouro (arXiv 2510.25741) is a fixed-size, overwritten residual vector — not an addressable memory. So within a single token's recurrence there is no substrate for revisiting. Any "revisit" would have to run through the cross-position KV cache, and even there Huginn shows earlier per-iteration entries are non-essential (cyclic overwrite `i mod k` down to budget 1 with minimal loss), while Ouro's per-loop KV matters (first-step-only reuse collapses GSM8K to 18.73 vs 78.92 full).

3. **Feed-back models (Coconut/CODI) are where a revisit test is even coherent**, because later thought/answer positions attend back to earlier thought positions through the KV cache. SCIT (2608.27265) already shows the causal carrier in CODI-GPT2 is the value-cache suffix trajectory — the closest existing thing to a revisit substrate — but it tests arithmetic transfer, not answer reversal.

4. **The BFS/superposition story is genuinely contested.** Observable Patterns (2606.12689) and the researcher's own COCONUT finding both argue the readout patterns don't reflect genuine latent-space search; Cui & Ye and Reasoning-by-Superposition (2505.12514, theory) argue they do. The disagreement is real and unresolved.

5. **The CoT-side toolkit is mature and directly portable.** Counterfactual resampling (Thought Anchors 2506.19143; Thought Branches 2510.27484), attention suppression, receiver heads, steering vectors for backtracking (Venhoff 2506.18167; Ward 2507.12638; The Value Axis 2606.17056), and hidden-state probes that predict correctness before the answer forms (Zhang 2504.05419) all have obvious latent analogues that have not been run.

### Section 2 — Architecture-level analysis (prose)

The central architectural fact, confirmed from the primary papers, is that **"revisiting" an earlier latent state is not uniformly possible, and in the flagship depth-recurrent models it is essentially impossible for a token's own prior iterations.** Two distinct channels must be separated.

**Channel A — a token's own earlier-iteration latent (the recurrent state).** In Huginn-3.5B (arXiv 2502.05171; HF `tomg-group-umd/huginn-0125`, 3.5B params, 800B tokens, architecture of 2 prelude / 4 recurrent / 2 coda blocks, improving up to ~num_steps=64) the update is sᵢ = R(e, sᵢ₋₁): a fixed-size vector overwritten each iteration, with the same injected input embedding e re-added every step, recurrence "in depth rather than time," and a fresh random s₀ per token. In Ouro (2510.25741; open 1.4B/2.6B LoopLMs trained on 7.7T tokens, matching up to 12B models, using 4 recurrent steps) the hidden state is likewise carried forward through the loop as a fixed-width vector with no auxiliary memory ("Universal Transformers Need Memory," 2604.21999, states Ouro "carries information through the hidden state alone"). In both, **a later iteration cannot attention-address the same token's earlier iterations as distinct stored objects** — they are consumed, not stored. So the intra-token substrate needed for literal "revisiting" does not exist in either model. This is the single most important fact for the project: within a token's recurrence, latent backtracking can only be **override** (further computation changes the state), never revisit.

**Channel B — other sequence positions' per-iteration KV.** Here the models diverge. **Ouro retains a per-loop KV cache that grows linearly with loop depth** — each of its 4 loops writes its own K/V. The Ouro paper (2510.25741, Table 14) reports that *reusing only the first step's cache* produces "a catastrophic performance collapse (e.g., 18.73 on GSM8K, down from 78.92)," while *last-step reuse* is nearly lossless (78.85 vs 78.92). So the intermediate-loop KV genuinely carries information that the first step alone does not. (A stronger "final-loop reuse collapses to 0" claim appears only in the third-party follow-up MELT, 2605.07721, which itself notes the discrepancy is "partly due to their few-shot CoT evaluation setup" — treat that as contested secondary evidence, not an Ouro-author claim.) **Huginn writes per-iteration KV by default but is architecturally overwrite-tolerant:** attention reads "the last, deepest available KV states," and the paper's zero-shot KV-cache sharing overwrites entry `i mod k` (e.g., budget 16, or even 1) with essentially no MTBench/GSM8K loss. So in Huginn earlier per-iteration KV entries are largely non-essential, whereas in Ouro they are load-bearing. Practically: a cross-position "revisit" test is far more likely to find something in Ouro than in Huginn.

**Feed-back models (Coconut, CODI, KaVa, PCCoT, SoftCoT, CoLaR).** These insert K latent positions between prompt and answer and feed each hidden state back as the next input embedding — as Coconut's own description (2412.06769) puts it, "we feed it back to the model as the next input embedding directly in the continuous space," enabling "a breadth-first search (BFS) rather than committing prematurely to a single deterministic path as in CoT." Because they are ordinary causal transformers over those positions, **later thought positions and the answer tokens do attend back to earlier thought positions through the KV cache** — so a "revisit" is at least coherent here. SCIT (2608.27265) is the only paper to causally localize this: on CODI-GPT2 the counterfactual computation transfers primarily through the **value-cache suffix trajectory**, not hidden states or keys. Dynamics within Latent CoT (2602.08783) independently finds non-local/routed step-to-step effects. But no feed-back paper has run an attention-attribution test asking specifically whether an answer reversal is driven by re-attending to a specific earlier thought position (revisit) versus later thoughts overwriting the running answer (override). (For reference on scale: Coconut reaches GSM8K 34.1% vs CoT 42.9% but with ~8.2 vs 25.0 forward passes, and ProsQA 97.0% vs CoT 77.5% — its efficiency edge is real, its raw-accuracy edge task-dependent.)

**Cross-family monitorability comparison.** Observable Patterns (2606.12689) and Ulterior Motives (2604.23460) both frame the safety/monitorability problem — latent models remove the human-readable trace — but neither directly compares Huginn-style depth-recurrent vs. Coconut-style feed-back affordances head-to-head. That comparison is itself an open niche.

### Section 4 — Gap analysis (prose)

**(a) Established causally.** That *some* latent steps are causally necessary and that step-to-step influence is **non-local/routed** rather than a clean chain (Dynamics, 2602.08783); that in feed-back models the causal carrier of counterfactual arithmetic is the **value-cache suffix trajectory** (SCIT, 2608.27265); that decodable BFS/superposition/scratchpad patterns are **not sufficient for mechanism** and appear in non-latent controls (Observable Patterns, 2606.12689); that latent-thought influence is **graded and low-rank**, not binary; and — architecturally — that Huginn's per-iteration KV is **overwrite-tolerant** while Ouro's per-loop KV is **load-bearing** (first-step-only reuse collapses GSM8K to 18.73 vs 78.92).

**(b) Shown only by readout.** The headline existence claim of latent backtracking itself (Cui & Ye, 2602.08100) — decoded belief flips in Huginn; the explore-then-verify entropy story (LaTER, 2605.07315); correct/incorrect trajectory-geometry differences (Tracing the Traces, 2510.10494); that latent states encode upcoming-answer correctness (the CoT-side Zhang 2504.05419, not yet run on latent models). None show the decoded belief causally drives the output.

**(c) Claimed but contested.** Whether Coconut's superposition/BFS is genuine latent search (Cui & Ye + Reasoning-by-Superposition 2505.12514 say yes-ish; Observable Patterns + Zhang 2025a/Rizvi-Martel 2026 + the researcher's own COCONUT preprint say it's shortcut/artifact). Whether backtracking is net-beneficial at all is contested even on the CoT side (Qin et al., 2504.07052).

**(d) Untested.** (i) **Revisit vs. override** — no latent paper tests whether a later step retrieves/attends to a specific earlier latent state versus overwriting the answer. (ii) **Existence/steerability of a latent "backtracking direction"** — established on CoT (Venhoff, Ward, Value Axis, SAE-steering) but never extracted in Huginn recurrent-state or Coconut thought space. (iii) **Predictability of an upcoming reversal from latent states** — CoT probes predict future correctness, but no one probes a latent model to predict a decoded reversal before it happens. (iv) **Head-to-head architecture-family comparison** of revisit affordances and monitorability.

**Project ratings.**
- **"Revisit vs. override across architectures": HIGH novelty, LOW crowdedness.** No paper addresses it; the architectural asymmetry (Huginn overwrite-tolerant vs. Ouro load-bearing KV; feed-back models genuinely attend back) gives a clean, falsifiable, causal experimental design using existing tools (attention suppression per Thought Anchors, resilience/Counterfactual++ per Thought Branches, cache-interchange per SCIT / 2608.04893). **Recommend pursuing.** The main redundancy risk is SCIT (2608.27265) and Dynamics (2602.08783) — they own the "causal carrier" and "routed propagation" framings, so the project must foreground the *revisit-vs-override* question specifically (does an answer reversal require re-reading a specific earlier state) rather than generic causal localization.
- **"Latent backtracking direction": MODERATE novelty, MODERATE-HIGH crowdedness.** The CoT-side direction work is mature (Venhoff, Ward, Value Axis, SAE-steering, Internal-states-before-wait), and Ulterior Motives + Observable Patterns already probe/steer latent thoughts. A pure "find and steer a backtracking direction in Coconut" result risks being seen as a port. It becomes novel if fused with the revisit/override question (e.g., does steering the direction cause re-attention to an earlier state, or pure override?).

**Redundancy flags (2026 papers to read before committing).** SCIT (2608.27265) — closest existing causal cache work; Dynamics within Latent CoT (2602.08783) — owns routed/non-local propagation; Observable Patterns (2606.12689) — owns the controls-and-causality critique and will be the referee lens; When Does Latent Communication Pay (2608.04893) — the cache-derangement methodology you'd reuse; The Value Axis (2606.17056) — closest to a steerable backtracking direction. None forecloses the revisit-vs-override question, but a reviewer will expect them cited and differentiated.

**Open-weight models & codebases at 1B–8B.** Huginn-3.5B (`tomg-group-umd/huginn-0125` HF weights + `wenquanlu/huginn-latent-cot` and `flowshu/latent-reasoning-interpretability` code); Ouro 1.4B/2.6B LoopLM (open weights, 7.7T-token, 4 recurrent steps); Coconut (Meta FAIR, `facebookresearch/coconut`); CODI (reproduced on GPT-2/Llama3-1B/Qwen3-4B in 2602.08783); Reasoning-by-Superposition (`Ber666/reasoning-by-superposition`); retrofitted recurrence (2511.07384). For CoT-side tooling: Thought Anchors (`interp-reasoning/thought-anchors`), steering-reasoning (`corl-team/steering-reasoning`).

## Recommendations
1. **Pursue "revisit vs. override," scoped first to feed-back models (Coconut/CODI at 1B–4B).** Only there does a within-sequence revisit substrate exist. Concrete first experiment: on a decoded belief-reversal case (Cui & Ye-style, but on Coconut), run Thought-Anchors attention suppression on each earlier thought position and Thought-Branches-style resilience/Counterfactual++ resampling on each latent step — if the reversal survives suppression of all earlier thought positions, it is **override**; if it collapses, it is **revisit-dependent**. This directly leverages SCIT's value-cache-suffix finding as the hypothesized substrate.
2. **Then test depth-recurrent as the negative/contrast case.** Predict — from the architecture — that Huginn shows override-only (per-iteration state overwritten; KV overwrite-tolerant) while Ouro *might* show cross-position revisit (load-bearing per-loop KV). Use Huginn's own cyclic-KV-budget knob (`i mod k`) as a built-in ablation. A clean result either way (recurrent models can't revisit; feed-back models sometimes do) is publishable.
3. **Fold the "backtracking direction" idea in as a sub-experiment, not the headline** — extract a direction (Venhoff/Value-Axis method) in thought space and test whether steering it induces *revisit* (re-attention) or *override*, tying the two project ideas together and defusing the crowdedness of a standalone direction paper.
4. **Benchmarks/thresholds that change the plan:** If SCIT or a new 2026 preprint already reports an attention-attribution revisit test on feed-back models → pivot to the architecture-family contrast as the headline. If Ouro's per-loop KV shows no cross-position answer-reversal dependence under interchange → drop the "Ouro can revisit" hypothesis and frame the paper as "latent backtracking is override, not revisit, across all current architectures," which is itself a strong claim. If a latent backtracking direction cannot be extracted with monotonic behavioral control (Venhoff's bar) → report the negative and lean on the causal suppression results.

## Caveats
- **Date/verification.** Several key items are 2026 preprints (2602.08100, 2602.08783, 2606.12689, 2608.27265, 2604.23460, 2606.17056, 2601.03595); some findings are read from abstracts/HTML rather than full PDFs (flagged in the tables).
